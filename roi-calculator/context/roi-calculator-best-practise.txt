1. Start with a real business question, not with features

Bad calculators feel like a disguised form. Good ones start with a question the visitor already has in their head, for example:

“Is AR-guided SOPs worth the hassle for us?”

“How fast do we earn back smart glasses + software?”

So:

Use a benefit-oriented title

“What could AR-guided SOPs save your plant?”

Not: “DeepSight ROI Calculator v1.0”

Use a one-sentence promise under it:

“Answer 6 quick questions and see your estimated savings, payback time, and 3-year ROI.”

That sets the expectation: I’ll get insight, not a pitch.

2. Give value quickly (and visibly) before asking for details

Leadgen temptation: gate everything behind an email wall.
Better: show value first, then ask for details to refine / export.

Practically:

Let the user see a first result without any email:

Annual savings (range or estimate)

Payback period

3-year ROI

Keep Step 1 super light:

of operators
of new hires

Error rate ballpark (or “I don’t know” with presets)

Show a live-updated result panel on the side:

As they type, let the headline change: “Estimated annual savings: € 180,000”

Only after they’ve seen something interesting you ask:
“Want a full report and detailed assumptions? → Leave your email and we’ll send it.”

This creates reciprocity: they got value, now they’re more willing to share.

3. Design the “lead moment” very deliberately

The lead capture should feel like a natural next step, not a trap.

Good patterns:

Soft gate for extra value, not for basic value

Basic view: big numbers + short explanation.

Premium view (behind email):

Detailed breakdown per benefit stream

Graph of cumulative cashflow

PDF export

Benchmark vs typical customers

Strong reason to submit (ActARion example):

“Compare your results to benchmarks in your industry”

If you want higher quality: add 1–2 qualifying questions.

5. Use the output as a conversation starter, not a verdict

The goal is not a “perfect” ROI, but a reason to talk.

So your output section should:

Highlight the big “wow” numbers

Annual savings

Payback (months)

3-year ROI (%)

Explain in plain language where that value comes from

“Most of your gains come from fewer errors and rework (62%).”

“Training and onboarding savings account for 25% of total benefit.”

Invite the user into the next step

“These numbers are based on your assumptions and typical AR impact in the industry.”

“In a 30-minute call we usually:”

Validate your error/downtime assumptions

Map 1–2 concrete AR use cases (e.g. changeovers on Line 3)

Build a pilot-level business case with your own SOP data

CTA examples:

Primary: “Review this business case with ActARion”

Secondary: “Download your ROI as PDF”

So the calculator is not the end of the story; it’s the beginning of the consultative sale.

6. Make it feel trustworthy and neutral

If the calculator always spits out “400% ROI in 6 months,” it loses credibility.

Good practices:

Show ranges instead of single magical numbers:

“Annual savings between €130k–€210k, depending on actual error rates.”

Let users tweak key assumptions and see what happens:

“What if error reduction is only 30%, not 50%?”

“What if you roll out to 1 line instead of 3?”

Show conservative defaults:

Use modest reduction percentages by default (e.g. -30% errors, -25% training time).

Label “ambitious” scenarios explicitly.

Add a short disclaimer:

“This calculator is for orientation only. In actual projects we refine these numbers using your real SOP data, OEE data, and scrap reports.”

This builds trust and positions you as a serious partner, not just a hype vendor.

7. Fit the calculator into your lead journey

A stand-alone calculator is nice, but the follow-up is what turns it into real pipeline.

Some best practices:

Tag all leads with context

Store key outputs in your CRM:

Estimated annual savings band

Main driver (training vs quality vs downtime)

Number of sites / operators

Their selected “timeline to implement”

Use personalised follow-up sequences

Email 1: send their PDF + short explanation of the biggest driver

Email 2: short case study aligned to that driver (e.g. “reducing changeover scrap with AR-guided SOPs”)

Email 3: invite to a 30-min “AR SOP ROI workshop” tailored to their plant

Route leads by size / potential

€250k potential saving/year → direct SDR / founder follow-up

€50k–€250k → email sequence + optional call

<€50k → nurture with content

For ActARion specifically, that could be:

“Future of Work with AR & AI” event invite

Case story: AR changeovers, AR-guided maintenance, etc.

Short DeepSight demo video linked in the email

8. UX & microcopy details that help

A few small, but important details:

Multi-step instead of a scary long single form

Progress indicator (“Step 2 of 4: Quality & downtime”)

Save input locally (so if they refresh, they don’t lose numbers)

Clear, non-technical labels:

“How many operators use these procedures?” instead of “N_op”

Friendly microcopy:

“Not sure? Pick the closest option – we’ll help refine this later.”
