# Designing AR training scenarios that fit your existing learning journey

Integrating AR training scenarios into your existing learning journey helps teams in industrial environments work safer, faster, and with fewer errors. The right approach ensures new technology fits your current processes and delivers measurable improvements in safety, quality, and workforce readiness.

## Why industrial training needs a new approach

Industrial operations face pressure to maintain high safety standards, reduce downtime, and transfer skills efficiently. Traditional training methods—classroom sessions, paper-based SOPs, and shadowing—struggle to keep pace with complex equipment, regulatory demands, and the need for rapid onboarding.

Common challenges include:
- Inconsistent knowledge transfer between experienced technicians and new hires
- Training content that quickly becomes outdated or hard to access
- High risk of errors when teams work from memory or incomplete instructions
- Limited ability to track training effectiveness or compliance in real time

For operations, maintenance, HSE, and L&D leaders, these issues translate into real risks: safety incidents, production delays, and increased costs. The need for more effective, scalable, and adaptive training is clear.

## Why change now: the case for AR-guided training

Augmented reality (AR) and AI-driven digital work instructions are transforming how industrial training is delivered. Instead of relying solely on classroom theory or one-on-one shadowing, AR training scenarios let learners practice directly on equipment, guided by step-by-step overlays and real-time feedback.

Key benefits include:
- Faster onboarding: New operators reach competence in days instead of weeks.
- Reduced errors: Visual, context-aware instructions minimize the risk of mistakes.
- Improved retention: Hands-on, scenario-based learning leads to better recall.
- Scalable knowledge capture: Expert knowledge is documented once and reused across sites and shifts.

These benefits are especially important as experienced workers retire and skills gaps widen.

## Mapping AR training to your existing learning journey

Rather than replacing your current training approach, AR scenarios should complement and enhance it. Here's how to map AR to a typical industrial learning journey:

### 1. Orientation and safety basics

New hires often start with safety inductions and general orientation. AR can support this stage by:
- Delivering standardized safety briefings with interactive checklists
- Guiding walk-throughs of key facilities and emergency procedures
- Tracking completion for compliance records

### 2. Theoretical foundations

Before hands-on work, many roles require background knowledge—understanding equipment, processes, or regulations. AR can:
- Overlay technical information on real equipment for contextual learning
- Provide interactive modules that blend theory with on-the-job visuals
- Allow learners to explore systems at their own pace

### 3. Supervised practice

Supervised practice is where AR shines. Trainees use AR-guided work instructions to complete tasks under observation, with step-by-step overlays and real-time validation. Benefits include:
- Consistent guidance regardless of which supervisor is on shift
- Immediate feedback if steps are missed or done incorrectly
- Reduction of time trainers spend on repetitive explanations

### 4. Independent work with support

Once trainees move to independent work, AR continues to provide just-in-time support. Workers can access AR SOPs for rare or high-risk procedures, refreshing their knowledge without returning to the classroom.

### 5. Continuous improvement and upskilling

Training doesn't end after onboarding. AR scenarios support ongoing skills development by:
- Delivering updates when procedures change
- Offering scenario-based drills for emergency response or quality checks
- Tracking skill gaps and recommending targeted learning

## Designing effective AR training scenarios

Not all training scenarios are equally suited for AR. Consider the following principles when designing AR training content:

### Focus on high-impact, high-risk tasks

Prioritize procedures where errors have significant consequences—safety-critical steps, complex assemblies, or tasks with high variation. AR's value is greatest where the cost of mistakes is highest.

### Keep scenarios modular

Break training into discrete, reusable modules that can be combined for different roles or skill levels. This makes it easier to update content and tailor learning paths.

### Integrate with existing LMS or training systems

Ensure AR scenarios connect to your learning management system (LMS) or training platform. This allows for unified tracking, reporting, and compliance documentation.

### Involve subject matter experts

Capture knowledge directly from experienced technicians. AR content authoring tools make it easy for SMEs to record best practices, annotate steps, and share expertise.

### Test with real users

Pilot AR scenarios with a small group of trainees and supervisors before full rollout. Gather feedback on usability, clarity, and impact, then iterate before scaling.

## Overcoming common barriers

| Barrier | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| "We already have a training program" | Position AR as a complement, not a replacement. Show how it fills gaps. |
| "Our trainers resist new tools" | Involve trainers early. Show how AR frees them for higher-value coaching. |
| "Content takes too long to create" | Use AI-powered authoring tools to accelerate content creation. |
| "We can't measure the impact" | Define KPIs upfront. Use analytics from AR platforms to track progress. |

## Getting started

Start by mapping your current learning journey and identifying stages where AR can add the most value. Engage stakeholders from operations, training, and IT to align on goals and success metrics. Pilot a small number of AR training scenarios before expanding.
