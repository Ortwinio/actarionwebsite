# Digital vs paper work instructions: which works better?

> Digital vs paper work instructions: digital wins when instructions change often, when tasks are complex or safety-critical, and when you need proof of execution. Paper still works for simple, stable tasks. Actarion converts your existing SOPs into digital, AR-guided work instructions that log every step for audits.

![Pen drawing of a paper clipboard instruction next to a tablet showing a digital work instruction step](/img/illustraties/compare-digital-vs-paper-work-instructions.svg)

## What goes wrong with paper instructions?

- The binder at the machine is two revisions behind the master file.
- Operators skip pages they think they know, and errors follow.
- New hires need a buddy for weeks because the text lacks context.
- Before an audit, you hunt for signed sheets and missing initials.

## Conclusion first

Paper is cheap to start and easy to read, but it cannot tell you whether a step was done. Digital work instructions cost effort to set up, then pay back through fewer errors, faster onboarding and automatic records. For most regulated or high-mix operations, digital is the better default.

## Digital vs paper: comparison table

| Criterion | Paper / PDF | Digital work instructions | Digital + AR guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Version control | Manual; old copies stay in circulation | One central version, pushed to every device | Same, plus visual overlays updated centrally |
| Clarity | Text and static photos | Steps with photos, video, checks | Step shown on the actual machine part |
| Error prevention | Depends on discipline | Mandatory confirmations, input checks | Location-specific guidance reduces wrong-part errors |
| Proof of execution | Signatures, if filled in | Time-stamped log per step | Log per step, including deviations |
| Onboarding | Buddy plus reading | Self-paced on the line | Hands-on guidance at the workstation |
| Language | One language per copy | Multiple languages per instruction | Same, with fewer words needed |
| Set-up effort | Low | Medium (faster with AI authoring) | Medium to high per use case |

## How do the criteria play out on the floor?

**Version control.** Paper copies drift. A digital instruction has one live version, so a change after a CAPA reaches every station the same day.

**Clarity.** A photo beats a paragraph; a highlight on the real valve beats a photo. That is the step from digital to AR.

**Error prevention.** Digital steps can require a confirmation, a measured value or a photo before the operator continues. Paper cannot block a skipped step.

**Proof of execution.** Every digital execution records who did what and when. That is your audit trail without extra paperwork.

**Onboarding.** Guided steps let new operators work earlier with less buddy time. Expected gains depend on the task: [CIJFER TE BEVESTIGEN].

**Set-up effort.** Writing digital instructions from scratch is slow. Converting existing SOPs with AI shortens that step considerably.

## When do you choose what?

1. **Stay on paper** for simple, stable tasks with low risk and no audit requirement.
2. **Go digital** when instructions change often, you run many product variants, or auditors ask for proof.
3. **Add AR** for complex assembly, changeovers, maintenance and safety procedures such as LOTO, where finding the right spot matters.
