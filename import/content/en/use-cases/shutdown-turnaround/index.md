# Shutdown turnaround planning: from work package to live progress

> A shutdown or turnaround is a planned stop of a plant to carry out maintenance, inspections and modifications in a short window. Actarion supports shutdown turnaround planning and execution with digital work packages: every crew follows the same AR-guided steps, progress is visible live and every task is documented for handover.

![Pen drawing of a process column with scaffolding and a progress dashboard for shutdown turnaround planning](/img/illustraties/use-cases-shutdown-turnaround.svg)

## What makes a turnaround hard?

- Hundreds of tasks in days, with many contractors who do not know your site.
- Paper work packs get lost, wet or are filled in afterwards.
- Progress reporting lags behind, so delays show up too late.
- Rework after start-up because a flange or valve was not done to spec.

## What do you measure?

| KPI | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Tasks completed on schedule | Grip on the critical path |
| Shutdown duration vs plan | Overall result |
| Rework and leaks at start-up | Quality of execution |
| Safety incidents | Control of high-risk work |

## How does a digital turnaround work?

1. **Build work packages** from your planning and standard procedures, with AI support.
2. **Assign to crews** with the right qualifications from the skills matrix.
3. **Brief with visuals.** Crews see the task, isolation points and hazards before they start.
4. **Execute with AR.** Steps such as flange tightening sequences or valve checks show on the equipment.
5. **Report live.** Each completed step updates the progress dashboard.
6. **Hand over with proof.** The record per task supports the start-up decision.

## How to start a pilot

Start with one recurring task type in your next shutdown, such as flange management or valve overhaul. We digitize those packages and measure progress and rework. [Plan your pilot](/en/pilot/).

## Common mistakes in a pilot

- **Starting during the turnaround itself.** Test the digital work packs on a smaller stop first, so nobody learns a new tool under peak pressure.
- **Too many work packs at once.** Pick the critical or repeated jobs, not all of them.
- **No plan for contractors' devices and access.** Arrange logins and hardware well before the first day.
- **Not closing the loop.** Use the recorded findings to update the work packs for the next turnaround.

### How do contractors get access?
They get user accounts for the duration of the shutdown `[TE BEVESTIGEN: licentiemodel tijdelijke gebruikers]`.

### Can we reuse packages next time?
Yes. Packages and lessons learned remain available for the next turnaround.
