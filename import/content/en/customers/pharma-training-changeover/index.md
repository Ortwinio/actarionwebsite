# Pharma case: AR training for changeovers on every shift

> An international pharmaceutical company used AR work instructions so night and weekend shifts could perform changeovers correctly without an expert on site. On the DeepSight platform it built 30 training courses that 350 employees can use, with knowledge capture and skills management. Actarion delivers this approach to pharma and biotech sites in Europe.

![Pen drawing: pharmaceutical filling machine with a cyan AR marker at the changeover, linked to a tablet](/img/illustraties/customers-pharma-training-changeover.svg)

*Case realised with technology partner DeepSight. Figures as published by DeepSight; customer anonymised.*

## At a glance

- **30** training courses built
- **350** employees can use the new training
- Modules: 3D instructions, knowledge capture, skills management

## Why did changeovers go wrong outside office hours?

- Night and weekend shifts struggled to perform changeovers correctly.
- Mistakes caused production delays and equipment breakdowns.
- Experts were not on site to help when it mattered.

## Starting situation

Changeovers on the production lines depended on a few experts. On night and weekend shifts, operators lacked support. The company needed to transfer operational knowledge and connect workers with remote help.

## Approach

1. Capture how experts perform each changeover.
2. Turn it into visual, step-by-step instructions with 3D guidance.
3. Train operators on the instructions and evaluate their competence.
4. Record qualifications per operator in the skills matrix.
5. Extend line by line once the first line works.

## Results

| Training courses built | 30 |
| Employees who can use the training | 350 |

## FAQ

### Does this fit a GMP environment?
Digital instructions with version control and execution logs support GMP documentation. Validation follows your own quality system.

### Can we show auditors who was qualified?
Yes. The skills matrix shows per operator which instructions and evaluations they completed.

### Do we need AR glasses in a cleanroom?
Not necessarily. Many sites start with tablets; glasses help when both hands are needed.
