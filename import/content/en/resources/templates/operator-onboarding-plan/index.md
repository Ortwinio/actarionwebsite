# Operator Onboarding Checklist: Free 90-Day Plan for Production

> An operator onboarding checklist is a structured plan that takes a new production employee from day one to working independently. This free template covers pre-arrival, the first day, the first week and a 30-60-90 day plan with task sign-offs and reviews. Actarion shortens onboarding with AR-guided work instructions at the machine.

![A phone held up to a production hall shows AR steps and a confirmed check for operator onboarding checklist](/images/resources-templates-operator-onboarding-plan-1600.webp)

## Why does onboarding take so long?

- New operators shadow a colleague who has no time to explain.
- Every trainer teaches a slightly different method.
- Nobody knows when someone is actually qualified.
- New hires leave in the first months because they feel lost.

## What is an onboarding plan and when do you use it?

An onboarding plan lists everything a new operator must learn, who teaches it and when it is signed off. Use it for every new hire, temp worker and internal transfer. It links directly to your [skills matrix](/en/resources/templates/skills-matrix-template/): each signed-off task raises the level in the matrix.

## The operator onboarding checklist

**Header:** Name ______ · Role ______ · Line / area ______ · Start date ______ · Buddy ______ · Team leader ______

**Before day 1**

- [ ] Contract, ID and access badge arranged
- [ ] PPE ordered in correct sizes
- [ ] Buddy assigned and briefed
- [ ] Training plan (this document) prepared
- [ ] Locker, workwear and system log-ins ready

**Day 1**

- [ ] Welcome by team leader, site tour, emergency exits and assembly point
- [ ] Safety induction: house rules, hazards, PPE, incident reporting
- [ ] Hygiene / GMP rules (where applicable)
- [ ] Introduction to the team and the buddy
- [ ] Explanation of the shift pattern and breaks

**Week 1**

- [ ] Lockout/tagout awareness (operator may not apply locks until trained)
- [ ] 5S and workplace standards
- [ ] Quality basics: defects, reject handling, escalation
- [ ] Observe the main tasks with the buddy
- [ ] End-of-week check-in with team leader

## How do you fill in the onboarding plan?

1. **Before day 1:** most first-day frustration comes from missing PPE or log-ins. Tick these off a week in advance.
2. **Safety first:** no machine work before the safety induction. Record the induction date for the audit.
3. **Tasks:** use the same task names as your work instructions and skills matrix.
4. **Method:** every task needs a written or digital instruction. "Watch and learn" is not a method.
5. **Sign-off:** only a qualified trainer signs off, after observing the task done independently.
6. **Reviews:** hold them, even if things go well. They prevent early leavers.

## FAQ

### How long should operator onboarding take?
It depends on the process. Plan for 90 days to reach independence on core tasks and review at 30, 60 and 90 days.

### What should be on an onboarding checklist for manufacturing?
Pre-arrival tasks, safety induction, site rules, task training with sign-off, and scheduled reviews.

### Who is responsible for onboarding?
The team leader owns the plan; a trained buddy and qualified trainers deliver it; HR supports.

### Can temp workers use the same plan?
Yes, with a shorter list of tasks. The safety induction and sign-off rules stay the same.
