# Quality Inspection Checklist: Free Template for Manufacturing

> A quality inspection checklist defines which characteristics are checked, against which specification, with which gauge, how often and what happens when a part fails. ## Why do defects still reach the customer?

- "Visual check OK" is ticked without a clear acceptance criterion.
- Inspectors measure differently, so results are not comparable.
- NOK parts are reworked without anyone recording why.
- Measured values stay on paper and nobody sees the trend.

## What is a quality inspection checklist and when do you use it?

A quality inspection checklist is a structured list of product characteristics with their specifications and inspection methods. Use it at three moments: **first-article** after setup or changeover, **in-process** at set intervals, and **final** before release. In automotive, the characteristics usually come from the control plan (IATF 16949); in other sectors from drawings and customer specifications.

## How do you fill in the checklist?

1. **Characteristics:** start with critical and special characteristics from the drawing or control plan. Do not list everything; list what matters.
2. **Specification:** always nominal and tolerance, or a limit sample for visual checks. "Looks OK" is not a specification.
3. **Gauge ID:** record which gauge was used so you can trace results if a gauge is later found out of calibration.
4. **Values, not ticks:** write actual measurements. Values reveal drift before parts go out of tolerance.
5. **Reaction plan:** decide in advance what happens on NOK. Under pressure, people improvise.
6. **First-article:** never start series production before the first-article is released.

## FAQ

### What should a quality inspection checklist include?
Product and batch details, characteristics, specifications, methods and gauges, measured values, OK/NOK and a reaction plan.

### What is the difference between first-article and in-process inspection?
First-article inspection verifies the first part after setup. In-process inspection checks parts at set intervals during production.

### How often should in-process inspections be done?
Based on risk and process stability, defined in the control plan, for example every hour or every 50 parts.

### Should I record values or just OK/NOK?
Record values for measurable characteristics. Trends tell you when the process drifts.
