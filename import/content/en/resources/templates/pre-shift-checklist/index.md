# Pre-Start Checklist: Free Template for Every Shift and Machine

> A pre-start checklist is a short check an operator completes before starting a machine or line at the beginning of a shift. It confirms that guards and emergency stops work, the machine is in good condition, utilities and materials are ready and the previous shift's issues are known. Actarion makes it a guided digital check.

![Pen drawing of a machine control panel with gauges next to a tablet showing the pre-start checklist for the shift](/img/illustraties/resources-templates-pre-shift-checklist.svg)

## Why do problems show up an hour into the shift?

- A guard or E-stop fault is noticed only when someone needs it.
- Low oil or air pressure causes a stop that a quick check would have caught.
- The previous shift's problem was not handed over.
- The paper check sheet is ticked in one go without looking.

## What is a pre-start checklist and when do you use it?

A pre-start (or pre-shift) checklist is a daily operator check of safety, condition and readiness before production starts. It is a core part of autonomous maintenance in TPM (Total Productive Maintenance). For powered industrial trucks, OSHA 1910.178(q)(7) requires an examination before use at least daily. For other machines, a pre-start check is the practical way to catch defects early.

## The pre-start checklist

**Header:** Machine / line ______ · Shift ______ · Operator ______ · Date / time ______

**1. Handover**

- [ ] Shift handover read; open issues from previous shift known
- [ ] Any machine tagged out or under maintenance? If yes, do not start

**2. Safety**

| # | Check | Standard | OK | NOK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emergency stop buttons | Tested, machine stops | [ ] | [ ] |
| 2 | Guards and interlocks | In place, door opens = stop | [ ] | [ ] |
| 3 | Light curtains / scanners | Clean, functional | [ ] | [ ] |
| 4 | Warning signs and floor markings | Visible, undamaged | [ ] | [ ] |
| 5 | Required PPE | Available and worn | [ ] | [ ] |

**3. Machine condition**

| # | Check | Standard | OK | NOK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Leaks (oil, air, water, product) | None | [ ] | [ ] |
| 7 | Oil / lubricant levels | Between min and max | [ ] | [ ] |
| 8 | Air pressure | ___ bar ± ___ | [ ] | [ ] |
| 9 | Abnormal noise, vibration, smell at start-up | None | [ ] | [ ] |
| 10 | Belts, chains, tooling | No visible damage | [ ] | [ ] |
| 11 | Sensors and cameras | Clean | [ ] | [ ] |

**4. Readiness**

| # | Check | Standard | OK | NOK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | Work order and specification | Correct and current | [ ] | [ ] |
| 13 | Materials and packaging | Available, correct batch | [ ] | [ ] |
| 14 | Settings / recipe | Matches order | [ ] | [ ] |
| 15 | Work area | Clean, 5S standard OK | [ ] | [ ] |

**NOK action:** describe ______ · Reported to ______ · Machine started? [ ] Yes [ ] No

**Sign-off:** Operator ______ · Team leader (if NOK) ______

## How do you fill in the checklist?

1. **Machine-specific:** adapt items 6–11 to your machine. Add the actual values (pressure, levels) to the standard column.
2. **Order matters:** handover and safety first. Never start a machine with a safety NOK.
3. **Physically check:** press the E-stop, open the guard. A tick without a test is not a check.
4. **Escalate NOKs:** define which NOKs block start-up (all safety items) and which can be logged for maintenance.
5. **Keep it short:** 10 to 20 items, five to ten minutes. Longer lists get skipped.

## FAQ

### What should be on a pre-start checklist?
Handover, safety devices, machine condition, utilities, and readiness of order, materials and settings.

### How long should a pre-start check take?
Five to ten minutes for most machines. If it takes longer, split daily and weekly items.

### Who completes the pre-start checklist?
The operator who will run the machine, at the start of every shift or before first use.

### What if a check fails?
Safety failures block start-up until fixed. Other failures are reported and assessed by the team leader or maintenance.
