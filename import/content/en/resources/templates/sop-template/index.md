# SOP Template for Manufacturing: Free and Audit-Ready

> An SOP template (standard operating procedure template) is a controlled format that defines a process: its purpose, scope, responsibilities, procedure steps, records and revision history. ## Why do SOPs end up in a binder?

- They are written for the auditor, not for the person doing the work.
- Review dates pass without anyone noticing.
- Nobody can show who was trained on which revision.
- Operators follow the "real" method, not the written one.

## What is an SOP and when do you need one?

An SOP is a document that describes **what** must happen in a process, **who** is responsible and **which records** prove it was done. You need one for every process that affects product quality, safety or compliance. Task-level detail goes into a [work instruction](/en/resources/templates/work-instruction-template/), which the SOP references.

## The SOP template

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Header** | Title · SOP number · Revision · Effective date · Next review date · Page x of y |
| **2. Purpose** | Why this SOP exists (1–2 sentences) |
| **3. Scope** | Which sites, lines, products and activities are covered, and which are not |
| **4. Definitions** | Terms and abbreviations used |
| **5. Responsibilities** | Role → responsibility (see table below) |
| **6. Safety and prerequisites** | PPE, hazards, training required, materials and equipment |
| **7. Procedure** | Numbered steps (see table below) |
| **8. Records** | Forms, logs and where they are kept, retention period |
| **9. References** | Related SOPs, work instructions, standards |
| **10. Revision history** | Revision, date, change, author |
| **11. Approval** | Author, reviewer, approver: name, signature, date |

## How do you fill in the SOP template?

1. **Purpose and scope:** be explicit about what is out of scope. Most audit findings come from unclear boundaries.
2. **Responsibilities:** use roles, not names. People change, roles stay.
3. **Procedure:** one action per step, written as an instruction ("Verify…", "Record…"). Link to work instructions for detailed tasks.
4. **Records:** name every form or log that proves the step happened. Auditors ask for these first.
5. **Revision history and approval:** never overwrite. Every change gets a new revision and approval. In GMP environments this supports data integrity (ALCOA+).
6. **Review:** set a review interval, typically one to three years, or sooner after any process change.

## FAQ

### What should an SOP contain?
At minimum: purpose, scope, responsibilities, procedure, records, references, revision history and approval.

### How long should an SOP be?
As short as possible. Most manufacturing SOPs fit on two to five pages; detail belongs in work instructions.

### How often should an SOP be reviewed?
Set a fixed interval, commonly one to three years, and review immediately after process, equipment or regulatory changes.

### Is this SOP template GMP-compliant?
The structure follows common GMP practice. Compliance depends on your document control, approvals and training records.
