# What Is OEE? Overall Equipment Effectiveness

> OEE, or overall equipment effectiveness, measures how much of planned production time is truly productive. It multiplies availability, performance and quality. A low OEE points to stops, slow cycles or scrap. Actarion improves the human side of OEE with guided changeovers, standard work and faster fault handling.

![A phone held up to a control room shows AR steps and a confirmed check for what is oee](/images/resources-glossary-oee-1600.webp)

## Why does it matter on the shop floor?

- Machines are 'running' but output is far below plan.
- Nobody agrees on why the line lost an hour.

## What is OEE?

OEE is a KPI that shows which share of planned production time results in good output at full speed. OEE = availability x performance x quality.

Availability captures stops such as breakdowns and changeovers. Performance captures slow cycles and micro-stops. Quality captures scrap and rework. Each factor points to a different improvement lever.

## Key elements

1. Availability: run time divided by planned time
2. Performance: actual output divided by ideal output
3. Quality: good parts divided by total parts

## Example from the shop floor

A line runs 80% of planned time, at 90% of ideal speed, with 98% good parts. OEE is 0.80 x 0.90 x 0.98, about 71%.

## FAQ

### What is a good OEE score?

It depends on the process. Benchmarks vary; compare against your own baseline first.

### Which OEE factor do work instructions affect?

Mainly availability (changeovers, faults) and quality (errors, scrap).

### Is OEE the same as uptime?

No. Uptime is only part of availability; OEE also counts speed and quality.
