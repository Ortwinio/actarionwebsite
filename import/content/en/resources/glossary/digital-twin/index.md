# What Is a Digital Twin? Definition & Example

> A digital twin is a virtual representation of a physical asset, line or process that is kept in sync with real-world data. It is used to monitor, simulate and optimize. Actarion connects to this idea from the operator side: AR instructions anchored to the real machine, with execution data flowing back.

![Pen drawing for the glossary term digital twin: machine and its dashed digital copy linked by arrows](/img/illustraties/resources-glossary-digital-twin.svg)

## Why does it matter on the shop floor?

- Engineering models never reach the people on the floor.
- Data about the asset and about the work done on it are separate.

## What is a digital twin?

A digital twin is a digital model of a physical object or system that is linked to it through data, so the model reflects its current state.

Twins range from 3D models with live sensor data to full process simulations. A 3D model alone is not yet a twin; the live data link makes it one.

## Key elements

1. Physical asset with sensors
2. Virtual model updated by data
3. Insights and actions back to the asset

## Example from the shop floor

A pump's twin shows vibration and temperature. When a threshold is crossed, a maintenance task opens with AR steps on the real pump.

## FAQ

### Is a 3D model a digital twin?

Not on its own; it needs a live data link to the real object.

### Do I need a digital twin for AR instructions?

No. AR instructions also work without a full twin.

### Where are digital twins used?

In asset management, process optimization, design and training.
