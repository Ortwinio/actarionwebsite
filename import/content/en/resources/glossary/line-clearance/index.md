# What Is Line Clearance? Definition & Steps

> Line clearance is the documented check that a production or packaging line is free of materials, labels and documents from the previous batch before a new batch starts. It prevents mix-ups. It is critical in pharma and food. Actarion makes line clearance a guided digital checklist with photo proof.

![Pen drawing for the glossary term line clearance: empty conveyor with a highlighted inspection zone](/img/illustraties/resources-glossary-line-clearance.svg)

## Why does it matter on the shop floor?

- A leftover label from the previous batch causes a recall risk.
- Paper checklists are signed without really checking.

## What is line clearance?

Line clearance is a verification step between batches that confirms all previous product, components, labels and records have been removed from the line.

In GMP environments, line clearance is usually signed by the operator and verified by a second person or QA. It covers every station, including bins, under conveyors and printers.

## Key elements

1. Remove all materials and documents of the previous batch
2. Check every zone, including hidden spots
3. Sign and verify, then release the line

## Example from the shop floor

Before packaging a new batch, the operator checks each zone, removes remaining blister foil, and QA verifies with photos before release.

## FAQ

### Why is line clearance important?

It prevents product and label mix-ups, a major cause of recalls.

### Who signs line clearance?

Typically the operator, with verification by a second person or QA.

### Is line clearance only for pharma?

No. It is also common in food, cosmetics and medical devices.
