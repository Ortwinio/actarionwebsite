# What Is Poka-Yoke? Error-Proofing Explained

> Poka-yoke is the lean principle of error-proofing: designing a part, tool or process so that a mistake cannot happen or is detected immediately. Examples are keyed connectors and sensors that stop a press. Actarion adds digital poka-yoke: mandatory checks and confirmations inside the work instruction.

![A technician holds an iPad up to a robotic assembly line; the screen shows AR work instruction steps for what is poka-yoke](/images/resources-glossary-poka-yoke-1600.webp)

## Why does it matter on the shop floor?

- The same mistake comes back after every retraining.
- Errors are found at end-of-line inspection, not at the source.

## What is poka-yoke?

Poka-yoke is a Japanese term for 'mistake-proofing'. It is any mechanism that prevents an error or makes it visible at the moment it occurs.

Physical poka-yoke uses shape, fixtures or sensors. Procedural poka-yoke uses forced sequences and checks. Both remove reliance on memory and attention.

## Key elements

1. Prevention: the error cannot occur
2. Detection: the error is flagged at once
3. Reaction: the process stops or asks for correction

## Example from the shop floor

A plug fits only one way into its socket, so it cannot be connected wrongly. In a digital instruction, the next step stays locked until a torque value is entered.

## FAQ

### Who invented poka-yoke?

The concept is associated with Shigeo Shingo and the Toyota Production System.

### Can software be poka-yoke?

Yes. Forced sequences and input checks in digital instructions act as procedural poka-yoke.

### Does poka-yoke replace inspection?

It reduces the need for it, but critical checks usually remain.
