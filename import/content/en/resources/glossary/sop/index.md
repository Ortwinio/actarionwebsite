# What Is an SOP? Definition and Example

> An SOP, or standard operating procedure, is the approved, written description of how a recurring task must be done, by whom and with which checks. SOPs secure quality, safety and compliance. Actarion converts existing SOPs with AI into digital, step-by-step work instructions with AR guidance and an audit trail.

![A technician holds an iPad up to a production hall; the screen shows AR work instruction steps for what is an sop](/images/resources-glossary-sop-1600.webp)

## Why does it matter on the shop floor?

- SOPs sit in a folder nobody opens on the line.
- Auditors find that practice differs from the written procedure.

## What is an SOP?

An SOP is a controlled document that describes the standard way to carry out a process. It states the purpose, scope, responsibilities, steps and required records.

SOPs are the backbone of ISO 9001, GMP and safety management. They describe *what* must happen at process level. A work instruction then describes *how* one task is done, step by step.

## Key elements

1. Purpose and scope
2. Roles and responsibilities
3. Procedure steps, checks and required records

## Example from the shop floor

A food plant has an SOP for cleaning a filling line between products. It defines who cleans, which agents to use and which check releases the line.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between an SOP and a work instruction?

An SOP describes the process and responsibilities. A work instruction details how one task is done, step by step.

### Who approves an SOP?

Usually the process owner and quality assurance, following your document control procedure.

### How often should SOPs be reviewed?

At a fixed interval set in your quality system, and after every relevant change or deviation.
