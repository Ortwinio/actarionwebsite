# What Is SMED? Changeover Method Explained

> SMED, or single-minute exchange of die, is a lean method to shorten changeover time by moving work to before or after the machine stop and simplifying what remains. The goal is changeovers under ten minutes. Actarion supports SMED with digital, AR-guided changeover instructions and time logging per step.

![Pen drawing for the glossary term SMED: machine with a stopwatch and a shortened changeover arc](/img/illustraties/resources-glossary-smed.svg)

## Why does it matter on the shop floor?

- Changeovers take longer on night shift than on day shift.
- Every operator changes over in a different order.

## What is SMED?

SMED is a lean method, developed by Shigeo Shingo, to reduce changeover time. 'Single minute' means a single-digit number of minutes.

The core idea is to separate internal tasks (machine stopped) from external tasks (machine running). External tasks move outside the stop. Internal tasks are simplified, standardized and done in parallel.

## Key elements

1. Record the current changeover and split internal and external tasks
2. Convert internal tasks to external where possible
3. Standardize and streamline the remaining steps

## Example from the shop floor

A packaging line prepares the next format parts and film rolls on a cart while still running. At the stop, the team only swaps parts in a fixed order.

## FAQ

### What does SMED stand for?

Single-minute exchange of die: a changeover in under ten minutes.

### Is SMED only for presses?

No. It works for any changeover, from packaging lines to CNC machines.

### How do I sustain SMED results?

Standardize the new sequence in a work instruction and measure each changeover.
