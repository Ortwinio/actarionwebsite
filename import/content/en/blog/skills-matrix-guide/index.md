# Skills Matrix: How to Build One for Your Production Team

> A skills matrix is a grid that shows which employee is qualified for which task, and at what level. It helps you plan shifts, spot single points of failure and target training. ## Why do you need a skills matrix?

- On Monday morning two people are sick and nobody knows who else can run the filler.
- One senior operator is the only one who can do a critical changeover.
- An auditor asks for proof that the operator was trained on the current SOP version.
- Training budgets go to whoever asks first, not to the biggest gap.

## What is a skills matrix?

A skills matrix, also called a training matrix or competency matrix, lists people in rows and tasks or skills in columns. Each cell holds a level. Standards such as ISO 9001 require organizations to determine and retain evidence of competence; a skills matrix is the common way to show it.

A typical four-level scale:

| Level | Meaning | Can work… |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Not trained | Not at all |
| 1 | In training | Only under supervision |
| 2 | Qualified | Independently |
| 3 | Expert | Independently and can train others |

## How do you build a skills matrix in 6 steps?

1. **List the tasks, not job titles.** Use your SOPs and work instructions as the source: one column per critical task.
2. **List the people.** Per team or shift, including temps.
3. **Define the levels and criteria.** What exactly does someone need to show to reach level 2?
4. **Assess each person.** Through observation by a team leader or trainer, not self-assessment alone.
5. **Set the target per task.** For example: at least three people at level 2 per shift.
6. **Review monthly and plan training.** Empty cells against the target are your training plan.

## Skills matrix example

Packaging team, early shift (target: 2 qualified per task).

| Operator | Filler changeover | Labeler setup | CIP cleaning | Line clearance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| B | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| C | 1 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
| D | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| **Qualified (≥2)** | **2** | **2** | **2** | **3** |

What you learn: CIP cleaning relies on A and B. If one is absent, the target fails. Operator C is the obvious next trainee.

## Common mistakes

- **Columns that are too broad.** "Maintenance" says nothing; "replace conveyor belt" does.
- **Levels without criteria.** Then the matrix measures opinions.
- **Updating once a year.** By then it is wrong and nobody trusts it.
- **No link to SOP versions.** A new SOP version may require retraining.

## How do you measure success?

Measure before you change anything. Record a baseline for two to four weeks, then compare after the rollout using the same definitions. These KPIs are the most useful:

| KPI | What it tells you | How you measure it |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage per task | Whether every task has enough qualified people | Qualified people vs required per task and shift |
| Single points of failure | Where you depend on one person | Tasks with only one qualified person |
| Expired qualifications | Whether recertification keeps up | Qualifications past their renewal date |
| Planning conflicts | Whether the matrix helps scheduling | Shifts staffed with unqualified people |

Keep the list short: three KPIs that the team leader reviews every week beat ten that nobody looks at. Agree on the definitions with quality and finance up front, so the results hold up in a business case.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between a skills matrix and a training matrix?
They are often used interchangeably. A training matrix sometimes focuses on required courses; a skills matrix on demonstrated ability.

### Who owns the skills matrix?
Usually the team leader or production manager, with HR or L&D supporting the process.

### How many levels should you use?
Three to five. Four levels work well for most production teams.

### Can a skills matrix be used in an audit?
Yes. It shows how you determine competence. Combine it with training records as evidence.
