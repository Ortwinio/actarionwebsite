## Why is SOP writing a bottleneck?

- Engineers spend evenings rewriting procedures instead of improving the process.
- Hundreds of legacy PDFs exist, in different formats and languages.
- The only up-to-date knowledge is in the heads of experienced operators.
- New lines start before the instructions are ready.

## What does an AI SOP generator do?

An AI SOP generator reads input and proposes structure. It can split long text into steps, rewrite passive sentences into commands, suggest warnings, translate and fill a fixed template. It cannot know what is true on your shop floor. That is why the expert stays in the loop.

| Task | AI is good at | A human must |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Splitting text into steps, applying a template | Check the sequence is right |
| Language | Short, active, consistent wording; translation | Check technical terms and values |
| Safety | Suggesting where warnings may be needed | Decide on hazards and controls |
| Visuals | Proposing where a photo is needed | Capture the right photo at the machine |
| Approval | Nothing | Approve and release the version |

## How do you create SOPs with AI in 6 steps?

1. **Collect the source.** Existing SOPs, manuals, notes, or a video of the expert doing the task.
2. **Set the template.** Give the AI your fixed structure, such as the [SOP template](/en/resources/templates/sop-template/).
3. **Generate the draft.** Let the AI produce steps, warnings and checks.
4. **Review at the machine.** An expert walks through the draft at the workstation and corrects it.
5. **Add visuals and values.** Photos, torque values, tolerances from the real equipment.
6. **Approve, release and measure.** Release through your document control and track how it is executed.

## Example

Input: an 18-page supplier manual for a labeling machine, plus a 6-minute phone video of the setup.

AI draft output: 14 steps, two suggested safety warnings, a list of tools and three suggested checks.

Expert review at the machine: two steps were in the wrong order, one torque value was missing from the manual, one warning was not relevant. After corrections and photos, the instruction is released and executed on a tablet.

## Common mistakes

- **Publishing AI output unchecked.** Plausible text is not the same as correct text.
- **Uploading confidential documents to public tools.** Check where your data goes and who can use it.
- **Skipping the floor walk.** The draft must be tested where the work happens.
- **Treating AI as the end point.** An SOP nobody follows on the floor is still just a document.

## How do you measure success?

Measure before you change anything. Record a baseline for two to four weeks, then compare after the rollout using the same definitions. These KPIs are the most useful:

| KPI | What it tells you | How you measure it |
|---|---|---|
| Writing time per SOP | Whether AI really saves authoring effort | Hours from source material to approved draft |
| Review rounds | Quality of the AI draft | Number of review cycles before approval |
| Backlog of outdated SOPs | Whether you are catching up | Count of SOPs past their review date |
| Deviations per procedure | Whether the new SOP works on the floor | Deviations logged per 1,000 executions |

Keep the list short: three KPIs that the team leader reviews every week beat ten that nobody looks at. Agree on the definitions with quality and finance up front, so the results hold up in a business case.

## FAQ

### Can AI write an SOP on its own?
It can write a draft. A process expert must check content, safety and values, and the SOP must be approved through your document control.

### Is an AI-generated SOP compliant with GMP or ISO 9001?
Compliance depends on content, review, approval and records, not on who wrote the draft. Keep your normal approval process.

### What input works best?
Existing procedures plus a video of an expert doing the task. The more concrete the source, the better the draft.
