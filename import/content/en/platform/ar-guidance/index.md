# Augmented reality work instructions that show where to act

> Augmented reality work instructions place digital guidance on top of the real workplace. Instead of reading a manual, the operator sees which valve, bolt or connector comes next, right on the equipment. Actarion delivers AR work instructions on tablets and AR glasses, and records each confirmed step for the audit trail.

![Pen drawing: AR glasses show a cyan overlay ring and step card on the correct valve of a panel](/img/illustraties/platform-ar-guidance.svg)

## Where do operators go wrong today?

- They pick the wrong part, valve or connector because the drawing does not match what they see.
- They skip a step under time pressure, and nobody notices until rework or a stoppage.
- Rare tasks, such as an annual maintenance job, are done from memory.
- New hires need an experienced colleague beside them for every task.

## What is AR guidance?

AR guidance, short for augmented reality guidance, overlays instructions on the physical world through a camera or transparent display. On a tablet, the operator points the camera at the machine and sees markers on the screen. On AR glasses, the markers appear in the field of view, and both hands stay free.

Each step combines a short instruction, a visual cue on the equipment and a confirmation. That is the difference with a PDF: the operator cannot lose their place.

## How do augmented reality work instructions work?

1. **Anchor the instruction.** The author links steps to the real equipment, for example with a marker or a 3D reference. [TE BEVESTIGEN: supported anchoring methods]
2. **Open the task.** The operator selects the task on the tablet or glasses, or scans a code on the machine.
3. **Follow the overlay.** Arrows, highlights and step cards show what to do and where.
4. **Confirm each step.** By tap, voice or gesture, depending on the device. Photos can be added as evidence.
5. **Escalate if needed.** If something is off, the operator starts a [remote support](/en/platform/remote-support/) call.

## FAQ

### Do I need AR glasses for AR work instructions?
No. A tablet camera works well for most tasks. Glasses add value when both hands are busy.

### Does AR work in a noisy or dusty plant?
Industrial tablets and some glasses are built for that. Voice control may be limited in very loud areas, so tap or gesture is the fallback.

### Is AR hard to maintain?
No. Instructions are edited in a no-code studio and updates sync to all devices.
