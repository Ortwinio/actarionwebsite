# AR training for frontline workers: productive sooner, safer at work

> AR training is on-the-job training where augmented reality shows a new operator each step directly on the real machine, via tablet or AR glasses. Actarion combines AR training with AI-generated work instructions and a live skills matrix, so frontline worker training is faster, consistent and documented for every employee.

![Pen drawing: a control panel with an AR arrow showing the turning direction and a step card, linked to a skills matrix](/img/illustraties/solutions-frontline-training.svg)

## Why does frontline worker training take so long?

- New hires shadow an experienced colleague for weeks. That colleague is not producing in the meantime.
- Every trainer explains the job differently. Errors and bad habits spread.
- Classroom training and e-learning do not stick when the real machine looks different.
- Nobody has a current overview of who is qualified for which task.

## What is AR training?

AR training, or augmented reality training, is a way of learning in which digital instructions are placed over the real work environment. The trainee sees arrows, highlights and step cards on the actual machine, on a tablet or through AR glasses such as Apple Vision Pro or HoloLens 2.

Frontline worker training covers everyone who works on the shop floor or in the field: operators, technicians, inspectors and field service engineers. AR training lets them learn on the job, at their own pace, while the instruction stays the same for everyone.

## How do you implement AR training?

1. **Choose one role** with long onboarding or many errors in the first months.
2. **Measure the baseline:** days until working independently, trainer hours, early errors.
3. **Digitize the critical tasks** for that role and set up the skills matrix.
4. **Pilot with the next group of new hires.** See [start a pilot](/en/pilot/).
5. **Compare and scale** to other roles and sites.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating AR training as a classroom replacement only.** The value is in learning at the real workstation, during real work.
- **No sign-off criteria.** Define per task when someone counts as competent, so the skills matrix means something.
- **Training only the trainees.** Team leaders and buddies need to know how to coach with the instructions too.
- **Letting content age.** When the process changes and the instruction does not, trust is gone quickly.
- **Not measuring time to competence.** Without a baseline you cannot show the gain to HR and management.

## FAQ

### Is AR training better than classroom training?
For practical shop floor tasks, AR training lets people learn on the real machine in context. Classroom training remains useful for theory and safety basics.

### Do I need AR glasses for AR training?
No. You can start with tablets. AR glasses help when the trainee needs both hands for the task.

### How do I prove someone is qualified?
Completed instructions and checks update the skills matrix and are logged with date and result. That gives you evidence for audits.
